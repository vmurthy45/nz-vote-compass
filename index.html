How it works

  1. Every party position comes from that party's own policy pages, or the full policy documents linked from them, read on . Nothing comes from news coverage, voting records or guesswork.
  2. If a party hasn't published a position on a statement, it's marked no stated position and that statement doesn't count towards its score.
  3. Positions are coded supports or opposes when the policy says so directly, and leans when it's partial, conditional, or a goal to work towards.
  4. A party agrees with you when its position is within one step of your answer on the five-point scale, partly agrees at two steps, and disagrees at three or more.
  5. Your match is the average closeness across the statements you both have a view on, pulled towards 50% when it rests on few statements. Results based on fewer than 10 are marked low confidence. See how scores are worked out.
  6. Parties publish different amounts of detail, so each result shows how many statements it rests on. Check the sources and full platforms before you vote.
How is the score worked out, and what does “low confidence” mean?

Step 1: raw match. For each statement where a party has a published position and you gave an answer, we measure how close you are. The same answer scores 100%, opposite ends of the scale score 0%. The raw match is the average of those, with statements you marked as important counting double.

Step 2: allow for thin evidence. Parties publish very different amounts of detail. A party with positions on only a handful of statements can reach a very high or very low raw score by chance, because there were few chances to disagree. So the score you see is pulled towards 50% when it rests on few statements, and left almost unchanged when it rests on many.

The formula is shown = 50% + (raw − 50%) × n ÷ (n + 5), where n is the number of statements in common. Each result also shows its raw score.

Low confidence marks any party with fewer than 10 statements in common with you. That usually means the party hasn't published a position on most of these issues, not that it agrees or disagrees with you. Read its quotes in the statement-by-statement breakdown and check its full platform.
